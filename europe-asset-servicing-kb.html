01 · Market Sizing

Europe is the world’s deepest regulated fund market — and the rules governing it are being rewritten.

European UCITS + AIF net assets reached EUR 23.4 trillion at end-2024, up 13.1% YoY, inside a total AuM pool of EUR 33 trillion when discretionary mandates are included. Six regulatory regimes — DORA, MiCA, AIFMD II, CSDR Refit, EMIR Refit, SFDR — land on a 2025–2027 calendar that forces every custodian, administrator and depositary to re-paper clients and re-platform operations. Whoever sells European asset servicing over the next 24 months is selling into a compliance-driven mandate reshuffle.

02 · Custody Landscape

US banks dominate globally; French banks own the European custody book.

The custody market splits on a fault line: BNY, State Street, JPMorgan and Citi lead on global AUC/A, but the assets physically held in Europe concentrate in French G-SIB hands — BNP Paribas, CACEIS and Société Générale. KPMG’s 43-bank survey finds the top 10 banks hold >78% of depositary assets under depositary (AuD). This is a consolidating market: CACEIS absorbed RBC Investor Services; BNP Paribas is acquiring HSBC’s German custody and depositary business.

04 · Regulatory Radar 2025–2027

Six regimes, one calendar — the re-papering window.

DORA is already live (17 Jan 2025); AIFMD II transposition lands 16 Apr 2026; MiCA’s grandfathering ends 1 Jul 2026; CSDR Refit’s remaining provisions apply from 17 Jan 2026. Every EU financial entity must re-paper vendor contracts, rebuild ICT third-party registers and re-paper depositary terms. This is the demand pulse behind the mandate reshuffle.

17 JAN 2025 · LIVE
DORA — Digital Operational Resilience Act

Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 applied. ICT risk management, third-party risk, resilience testing, incident reporting and CTPP oversight. Registers of information due to ESAs by 30 Apr 2025. BaFin rescinded KAIT/VAIT/ZAIT circulars; BAIT fully repealed 31 Dec 2026. UK parallel: PS21/3 deadline 31 Mar 2025; PS24/16 critical third parties from 1 Jan 2025.

Source: ESMA; EUR-Lex; Jones Day; Morgan Lewis; Mayer Brown FACT
30 DEC 2024 · LIVE
MiCA — Markets in Crypto-Assets

Regulation (EU) 2023/1114. Phase 1 (30 Jun 2024): ART/EMT stablecoin issuer rules. Phase 2 (30 Dec 2024): full CASP authorisation regime. Grandfathering ends 1 Jul 2026. iXBRL white-paper format from 23 Dec 2025. Ireland set a 12-month transition ending 29 Dec 2025.

Source: EUR-Lex; ESMA; Central Bank of Ireland FACT
16 APR 2026 · TRANSPOSITION
AIFMD II — Alternative Investment Fund Managers Directive

Directive (EU) 2024/927. New loan-origination regime (leverage limits: open-ended 175%, closed-ended 300%; 20% single-borrower cap; 5% risk retention); depositary cross-border appointment; CSDs in custody chain; liquidity-management tools. Supervisory reporting from 16 Apr 2027. Grandfathering for loan-originating AIFs ends 16 Apr 2029. Depositary cross-border permitted only if home-state market < EUR 50 bn aggregate AUC.

Source: Clifford Chance; DLA Piper; Sidley; McDermott; Debevoise FACT
17 JAN 2026 · REMAINING PROVISIONS
CSDR Refit

Regulation (EU) 2023/2845. Mandatory buy-ins suspended — now a “last resort” the Commission may activate only if cash penalties fail AND fails threaten EU stability. ESMA to report on T+1 opportunity by 17 Jan 2025 and biennially after. Cash penalties live since 1 Feb 2022; ESMA consulting on rate increases (up to 6×, up to 25× after 6th fail day).

Source: Norton Rose Fulbright; Linklaters; SGSS FACT
29 APR 2024 / 30 SEP 2024 · LIVE
EMIR REFIT — Derivatives Reporting

EU EMIR new RTS/ITS from 29 Apr 2024; outstanding derivatives re-reported by 26 Oct 2024. UK EMIR from 30 Sep 2024; back-reporting ended 31 Mar 2025. ISO 20022 XML; +74 reporting fields; UPI mandatory; UTI generation rules.

Source: ESMA; FCA; Sidley; Taylor Wessing FACT
SINCE JAN 2021 · ONGOING
SFTR — Securities Financing Transactions Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2015/2365. Reporting fully applicable since Jan 2021 across banks, CCPs/CSDs, UCITS ManCos/AIFMs/insurers/pension funds, NFCs. ISO 20022 XML; validation rules updated March 2023.

Source: ESMA; FMA Liechtenstein FACT
IN REVIEW
SFDR — Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 remains in force; ongoing ESA consultations on PAI indicators and Article 6/8/9 fund classification; ESMA/ESA review ongoing. DATA GAP — latest 2025–2026 SFDR amendments not separately sourced in this pass; primary research recommended before client-facing use.

05 · Tokenisation & DLT

The legal rails for tokenised securities already exist — in Germany, Luxembourg and Switzerland.

Europe wrote the rulebook before the market. Germany’s eWpG (2021), Luxembourg’s blockchain laws (2019/2021) and Switzerland’s DLT Act (2021) each created a legally recognised electronic-security category. The ECB’s digital euro moved to its next phase on 30 Oct 2025, targeting potential first issuance in 2029. And the first fully tokenised UCITS fund on a public blockchain — Franklin Templeton, CSSF-approved Oct 2024 — is live in Luxembourg.

07 · Brexit Impact

332 firms relocated — Dublin won, and the OFR clock is ticking to Sep 2026.

New Financial counts 332 firms that moved business, staff, assets or legal entities from the UK to the EU post-Brexit. Dublin took the most (115), ahead of Luxembourg (71), Paris (69), Frankfurt (45) and Amsterdam (40). The UK’s Overseas Funds Regime (OFR) — replacing the Temporary Marketing Permissions Regime — staggers fund applications over two years ending September 2026, the next hard date in the cross-border distribution calendar.

08 · Buyer Maps

Four buyer types, four distinct trigger events.

European asset servicing is bought by asset managers, pension funds, insurers and sovereign wealth funds — each with a different decision unit, pain point and switching trigger. The 2025–2027 regulatory calendar lands on all four at once, but the conversion path differs: managers re-paper for AIFMD II/DORA; pension funds consolidate providers; insurers optimise for Solvency II; SWFs review on multi-year cycles.

10 · Six GTM Plays

Six plays — each anchored to a regulatory date or market event.

Each play is keyed to a dated trigger (AIFMD II transposition, DORA, ELTIF 2.0, OFR) or a structural shift (tokenisation, front-to-back consolidation, cross-border distribution). Click any card to expand the trigger, proposition, proof points and landmines.

1. AIFMD II Depositary Readiness

Trigger: 16 Apr 2026 · AIFMs / private debt
Buyer
AIFMs — especially loan-originating AIFs and private-debt managers.
Trigger
16 Apr 2026 transposition deadline; depositary cross-border appointment option; leverage limits (open-ended 175% / closed-ended 300%); new reporting obligations from Apr 2027.
Proposition
AIFMD II-compliant depositary and custody services with readiness for the loan-origination regime, CSD custody-chain integration and cross-border depositary appointment where the home market lacks capacity.
Proof points
Existing AIF depositary market position; regulatory reporting infrastructure; expertise in loan-origination fund structures.
Landmines
Grandfathering provisions (AIFs pre-Apr 2024 may not need immediate action); Luxembourg/Ireland depositary markets well-served (cross-border appointment unlikely to apply there); complex legacy loan portfolios.

2. DORA Operational Resilience & Third-Party Risk

Trigger: 17 Jan 2025 (live) · all EU financial entities
Buyer
All EU financial entities — banks, insurers, asset managers, AIFMs, UCITS ManCos — especially those relying on custodian/administrator platforms.
Trigger
DORA applied 17 Jan 2025; registers of information due to ESAs 30 Apr 2025; CTPP oversight designation ongoing.
Proposition
DORA-compliant ICT risk management, third-party risk monitoring, register-of-information maintenance, TLPT (threat-led penetration testing) support and contractual alignment.
Proof points
Demonstrable ICT risk framework; existing client onboarding/monitoring infrastructure; ability to provide DORA-required data exports.
Landmines
Many firms already compliant or using Big 4 consultancies; DORA scope ambiguity (especially sub-contracting chains); penalty regime not yet tested.

3. Tokenised Fund Infrastructure DLT

Trigger: LU/DE DLT law · Franklin Templeton live
Buyer
Asset managers exploring fund tokenisation (Franklin Templeton, Hamilton Lane, Partners Group, Schroders, abrdn).
Trigger
CSSF/Luxembourg DLT legal framework (2019/2021 laws); eWpG in Germany; growing institutional demand for fractionalised private-market access.
Proposition
End-to-end tokenised fund servicing — DLT-based transfer agency, on-chain share registry, custody of digital assets, integration with traditional fund admin.
Proof points
Franklin Templeton tokenised UCITS in Luxembourg (CSSF-approved); Hamilton Lane / Sygnum / Apex GPA Fund tokenisation; partnerships with tokenisation platforms (Tokeny, etc.).
Landmines
Early-stage market (limited AUM); regulatory uncertainty across jurisdictions; technology vendor lock-in; investor wariness of blockchain/crypto association.

4. ELTIF 2.0 / Private Markets Servicing

Trigger: ELTIF 2.0 (2024) · evergreen market +73%
Buyer
Asset managers launching ELTIF 2.0 funds (Partners Group, Fineco AM, others); institutional investors seeking private-market access.
Trigger
ELTIF 2.0 regulation (effective 2024) expanding retail access to private markets; European evergreen fund market grew 73% from ~EUR 54 bn (end-2024) to EUR 93.4 bn (3Q 2025).
Proposition
Full-lifecycle private-markets servicing — fund admin, depositary, TA, custody, valuation, regulatory reporting for ELTIF and evergreen structures.
Proof points
Northern Trust won Partners Group ELTIF 2.0 mandate; SGSS won Fineco AM Irish ELTIF; CACEIS/State Street alternatives capabilities.
Landmines
Complex valuation requirements; limited number of ELTIF-eligible distributors; competing platforms; need for alternatives expertise (not all custodians have this).

5. Consolidated Front-to-Back Outsourcing

Trigger: cost pressure · single-provider model
Buyer
Mid-to-large asset managers seeking operational simplification (e.g. First Sentier Investors).
Trigger
Cost pressure, legacy technology debt, desire for a single-provider model across multiple geographies and asset classes.
Proposition
Integrated front-to-back platform combining custody, fund admin, TA, middle-office, investment accounting, regulatory reporting and data services across multiple jurisdictions.
Proof points
Northern Trust won First Sentier Investors consolidated mandate (AU/IRE/SG/UK); State Street Alpha platform; BNY strategic-platforms transition.
Landmines
High switching costs and migration risk; client concerns about lock-in; need for multi-geography licensing; pricing pressure.

6. Cross-Border Fund Distribution Support (OFR / UCITS Passport)

Trigger: OFR effective Sep 2024 · transition to Sep 2026
Buyer
International (US/Asian) asset managers entering European markets; UK-based managers with EU-domiciled funds.
Trigger
UK Overseas Funds Regime (OFR) effective Sep 2024; MiCA creating new crypto-asset fund structures; growing demand for UCITS cross-border distribution.
Proposition
Domiciliation support (Luxembourg/Ireland), ManCo/hosting services, UCITS/AIFMD passport management, TA for cross-border distribution, regulatory reporting across EU jurisdictions.
Proof points
Luxembourg/Ireland as dominant domiciles (25% / 21% market share); white-label/platform solutions (HANetf, State Street, DWS); Apex Group GAM ManCo acquisition.
Landmines
Crowded market with established players; regulatory complexity varying by jurisdiction; ManCo consolidation/restructuring trends (some re-evaluating post-Brexit structures).
Sequencing: Plays 2 (DORA) and 1 (AIFMD II) are now plays — the deadlines are live or imminent. Plays 3–4 (tokenisation, ELTIF) are positioning plays for 2026–2027. Plays 5–6 (consolidation, distribution) are always-on structural plays. ASSESSMENT
