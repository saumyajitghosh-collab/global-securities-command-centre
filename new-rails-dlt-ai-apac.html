01 · Executive Thesis

The rails are being rebuilt — while the mandates are still in motion.

Two technologies are restructuring the economics of custody, fund administration and transfer agency at the same time. DLT changes what a record of ownership is. AI changes what it costs to operate one. APAC — not the US, not Europe — is where both are moving into production first. Whoever is selling asset servicing in this region over the next 36 months is selling into an infrastructure transition, and infrastructure transitions are when mandates move.

02 · Why Now

2026 is the year the pilots became plumbing.

For a decade, "blockchain in post-trade" was a conference topic. Three things changed: regulators built the rails themselves (EnsembleTX, Project Guardian, RBI's wholesale-CBDC pilots), the largest custodians moved core books-and-records on-chain (BNY's Digital TA), and AI moved from copilots to production agents with published cost numbers. The IMF now describes tokenisation as a structural redesign of financial market infrastructure, not incremental digitisation. FACT

Regulators are operating the testbeds DLT

HKMA's EnsembleTX pilot (launched 13 Nov 2025) settles real-value tokenised-deposit transactions through 2026, starting with tokenised money-market-fund transactions over the HKD RTGS, upgrading toward tokenised central-bank money. India's RBI launched a deposit-tokenisation pilot on 8 Oct 2025 using wholesale CBDC as the base layer, alongside tokenised certificates of deposit under its Unified Markets Interface concept. FACT

Incumbents moved core records on-chain DLT

BNY launched Digital Transfer Agency on 29 Jul 2026 — extending TA for its ~$8.6T fund-services business to digitally native funds, with legal representation of a fund's books and records on a public blockchain, across jurisdictions and chains. Deutsche Bank debuts digital-asset custody in 2026. When the reference books move, everything downstream (FA, recs, reporting) must follow. FACT

AI now has audited-sounding numbers AI

BNY's Eliza platform reports 117 AI solutions in production, a 40% reduction in NAV false positives, ~25% faster legal contract review, a digital employee processing ~2,000 payment repairs, and a ~5% unit-cost reduction on core custody trades. State Street's Alpha platform reported $380B of AuC/A wins with 28 clients live. Cost curves, not demos. FACT

Why this matters to a Head of Sales, specifically: every one of these programmes creates a switching event — a fund launching in tokenised form, a treasurer adopting tokenised MMFs as collateral, an insurer forced to re-platform because its administrator can't service on-chain assets. The prior module taught us to chase events, not AUM. DLT and AI are now the largest event generators in the region. ASSESSMENT
03 · Two Technologies, One P&L

DLT changes the product. AI changes the cost of producing it.

These get bundled as "digital", but they attack the P&L from opposite ends — and the winning propositions in APAC combine them. Keep the distinction sharp in every client conversation.

Distributed Ledger Technology DLT

What it really is: a shared, programmable record of ownership. In asset-servicing terms, it collapses the gap between the register (TA), the books (fund accounting) and the asset (custody) into one synchronised state.

What it changes: settlement becomes atomic (DvP without a two-day trust gap), reconciliation shrinks because participants share one ledger rather than comparing copies, fund units become transferable 24/7 collateral, and compliance can be embedded in the token itself (whitelisted wallets, transfer restrictions). New revenue lines appear: digital custody, token issuance agency, on-chain TA, smart-contract administration.

Artificial Intelligence — now agentic AI

What it really is: after RPA (deterministic clicks) and copilots (drafting help), agentic AI plans and executes multi-step workflows across systems — pulling data, applying policy, proposing actions, packaging audit evidence.

What it changes: the expensive "last mile" of operations — exceptions, breaks, investigations, corporate-action election chasing, NAV oversight, KYC refresh, client queries. That is precisely where asset-servicing margin dies today. It also changes the sales conversation: providers with production AI can price aggressively and still expand margin, and can sell "operations as an outcome" rather than headcount.

The convergence — where the real mandates sit CONVERGENCE

A tokenised fund generates on-chain events at machine speed, 24/7, across multiple chains. No human ops team can supervise that; only an AI-instrumented operation can. Conversely, AI agents need clean, canonical data — which a shared ledger provides. DLT creates the always-on market; AI makes it serviceable at cost. Propositions that offer one without the other will lose to those offering both. ASSESSMENT

06 · India Deep Dive

India is running a dual-track digital strategy — and GIFT is the fast track.

Onshore, the RBI is tokenising the money layer first (deposits, CDs, wholesale CBDC) while SEBI writes the AI rulebook. Offshore, IFSCA at GIFT City is building the asset layer — RWA tokenisation, its own depository, sandbox pilots. For a regional sales head, the two tracks produce different buyers and different clocks.

Track 1 — RBI: money first DLT

Deposit-tokenisation pilot launched 8 Oct 2025 with wholesale CBDC as the underlying settlement layer; a parallel pilot tokenises certificates of deposit, with commercial paper under exploration. Both sit inside the RBI's Unified Markets Interface (UMI) concept — tokenise financial assets, settle atomically in central-bank money. FACT

Read-through: India is building the settlement asset before the securities. When tokenised money-market instruments scale, custodians and administrators who can plug into UMI-style rails early will hold the field position. ASSESSMENT

Track 2 — IFSCA: assets at GIFT DLT

IFSCA's consultation on tokenisation of real-world assets remains India's only dedicated RWA policy document; its sandbox has already approved a live pilot (a ~US$7M commercial-property tokenisation wrapped in AIF units for accredited investors). A separate framework requires IFSC entities to dematerialise via the IFSC's own depository — India International Depository IFSC — with migration from domestic depositories by 31 Mar 2026. FACT

Read-through: GIFT is assembling a full offshore capital-markets stack — depository, ISINs, funds, tokenisation rules. That is a greenfield asset-servicing ecosystem with no incumbent digital provider. ASSESSMENT

Track 3 — SEBI: the AI rulebook AI

SEBI consulted on a Responsible AI/ML framework (Jun 2025) proposing a tiered, regulatory-lite regime for non-customer-facing uses, and is now converting it into guidelines. Chairman Pandey (CAPAM, 19 Aug 2026): every regulated entity remains fully responsible for any AI/ML tool it uses, in-house or procured; an IT-resilience index for market infrastructure is under examination. FACT

Read-through: accountability-without-prescription favours providers who arrive with governed, auditable AI — model registers, evidence trails, testing frameworks. "Our AI is SEBI-ready" becomes a differentiator when selling ops outsourcing to Indian AMCs, custodians and RTAs. ASSESSMENT

The Bill to watch: the Asset Tokenisation (Regulation) Bill, 2026 — introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 14 Mar 2026 as a private member's bill — proposes India's first dedicated RWA licensing regime with a multi-regulator model: securities tokens to SEBI, payment/stable-value tokens to RBI, sectoral tokens to IRDAI/PFRDA, with mandated coordination. Private members' bills rarely pass, but they reliably preview the architecture of eventual government legislation. Track it as a leading indicator, not a live rule. FACT ASSESSMENT
India's coordination plumbing already exists: the Inter-operable Regulatory Sandbox (IoRS) lets products spanning RBI / SEBI / IRDAI / PFRDA / IFSCA remits be tested under one process — exactly the shape a tokenised fund (security + payment + offshore) takes. Clients will need a partner who can navigate all five regulators at once. That is a services proposition in itself. FACT ASSESSMENT

13 · Risks & Honest Caveats

What would make this thesis wrong — say it before the client does.

Credibility with an MD audience comes from the caveats, not the hype. These are the honest counterweights.

Tokenised AUM is still a rounding error

~$39B of tokenised RWA against ~$130T+ of global fund assets. BUIDL even saw a double-digit monthly AUM decline in mid-2026. The growth rate is the story, not the stock. Never present tokenisation as a current revenue pool; present it as an option-value and switching-event machine. FACT ASSESSMENT

Old rails won't die — they'll coexist for years

BNY itself says traditional TA systems will run alongside blockchain rails for years, with trillions staying on existing infrastructure. The realistic sale is hybrid servicing, which is harder operationally than either pure world — and that difficulty is precisely the service opportunity. FACT

India onshore has no securities-tokenisation law

The 2026 Bill is a private member's bill; SEBI has issued no tokenised-securities framework; RBI is cautious on private stablecoins. Domestic promises must stay at the money layer; asset-layer plays live at GIFT. Overselling onshore India tokenisation would be lethal to credibility. FACT ASSESSMENT

AI numbers are vendor-disclosed, not audited

The −40% / −5% figures come from BNY's own disclosures; peers disclose less, making comparison hard. Quote them as "published by BNY", never as industry benchmarks. SEBI-style accountability also means AI failures land on the client — governance is a genuine cost, not a slide. FACT

Smart-contract and bridge risk is real

Cyber risk, contract bugs and cross-chain bridges are named risks even by the banks deploying them. Insurance, key management and incident playbooks belong in every digital-custody proposal. FACT

Fragmentation could stall everything

Six jurisdictions, dozens of chains, competing standards (Guardian vs Ensemble vs Progmat vs UMI). The SFC itself flags interoperability as the scaling constraint. If corridors don't connect, tokenised assets stay siloed and the TAM shrinks. Watch the HK–SG corridor as the leading indicator. FACT ASSESSMENT
