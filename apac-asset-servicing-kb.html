Folio 01 · The Market

The APAC opportunity — and how to capture it

Three forces are converging to make APAC the highest-growth region globally for asset servicing. The incoming MD inherits a market in structural transformation — with clear white space for a technology-forward, relationship-driven player.

Structural growth

Fastest-growing region globally

APAC custody revenue ~$7.9B (23% of global), growing at 10.9% CAGR through 2031 — outpacing North America and Europe. AUM across 8 key markets exceeds $23.8T, driven by Japan's NISA reform, India's MF/AIF explosion, and Australia's superannuation system.

Competitive disruption

Globals retreating from local sub-custody

JPMorgan exited local custody in HK, Taiwan, Australia and Korea — outsourcing ~$520B to HSBC and StanChart. State Street acquired Mizuho's global custody business (~$850B AUC). The market is bifurcating: global custody + data platforms at the top, local sub-custody partnerships below.

Outsourcing acceleration

APAC clients outsource more, faster

61.9% of APAC asset managers plan to increase outsourcing after initial experience (vs 44.5% in US/Europe). Middle-office outsourcing is a $1.9B APAC market growing to $4.5B by 2033 at 11.2% CAGR. Drivers: efficiency (70%), operational expertise (68.5%), cost (61.9%).

Key thesis for the new MD: The APAC market is at an inflection point. Global custodians are bifurcating — retreating (JPM local custody) or consolidating (State Street–Mizuho). Clients increasingly want front-to-back platforms, not just custody. The winning play: position as the technology-forward alternative to the big three — competing on data solutions, digital-asset readiness, private-markets servicing, and local-market depth in India and Southeast Asia where the big three are under-indexed.

Where the big three are vulnerable

  • State Street — Alpha platform conversion is slow (25 of 35 clients live); concentration risk in a few mega-mandates; late to digital-asset custody.
  • BNY — Under-indexed in APAC custody share (ranked ~#11 in Australia AUC); corporate-trust strength less differentiated in APAC than in the US.
  • JPMorgan — Full exit from APAC local custody creates dependency on HSBC/StanChart sub-custody; not seen as a digital-asset custody leader.

Where to attack

  • India — MF AUM ~US$1T (₹85.8 lakh cr, Jul 2026); AIF commitments ₹16.94 lakh cr (~$190B); SEBI's Sep 2025 custodian overhaul (net worth to ₹75 cr, financial services as permitted activity) opens integrated servicing.
  • Australia super — A$4.2T system; CPS 230 (Jul 2025) forcing the decade's biggest third-party risk review; funds insourcing PM but buying technology and outsourced trading.
  • Singapore VCCs — ~1,406 VCCs / 3,443 sub-funds; alternatives AUM ~US$1T; MAS outsourcing overhaul creating compliance demand.
  • Japan NISA — Retail fund inflows ¥11.6T in FY2024 (+160% YoY); structural savings-to-investment shift.
  • Digital assets — SG DTSP (Jun 2025), HK VASP/ASPIRe, Japan stablecoin rules: regulated custody is a greenfield.

Folio 03 · The Market

Eight markets, eight playbooks

Each APAC market has a distinct regulatory architecture, fund-domicile ecosystem and client concentration. Switch tabs for country-level data on AUM, regulatory drivers, and where the firm wins.

Market snapshot — Asia's alternatives hub

  • Total AUM S$6.07T (US$4.46T), +12% YoY — first time above S$6T; net inflows S$290B, +50% YoY.
  • Alternatives AUM S$1.39T (~US$1.0T), +14% YoY; private credit +21%.
  • 1,298 licensed/registered fund management companies; ~1,406 VCCs with 3,443 sub-funds (end-2025), ~S$220B+ estimated AUM. Strategy mix: PE/VC 39%, EAM/MFO 22%, hedge 19%, traditional 15%, RE 4%.
  • 76% of AUM sourced from outside Singapore; 88% invested overseas; ESG overlay on 48% of AUM.

Regulatory & structural drivers

  • MAS outsourcing framework (Dec 2024): two-track regime with MOORS — semi-annual registers, 30-day prior consent for subcontractors, mandatory contract clauses, 3-year audit cycles.
  • TPRM escalation (Jul 2025): post-ransomware legacy contract remediation; board-level accountability.
  • DTSP regime (Jun 2025): digital token service provider licensing under the FSM Act — MAS "generally not granting licences", extremely restrictive.
  • ISSB climate disclosures: listed companies from FY2025; large non-listed from FY2027 — first in Asia.
Opportunity: The premier hub for alternative-fund servicing in APAC. The VCC regime created 3,443 sub-funds needing custody, fund admin and TA; MAS's intensified outsourcing/TPRM regime creates compliance-driven demand for servicers with strong governance. Even BlackRock has been recruiting a Transfer Agency Services Director APAC in Singapore — a demand signal from the very top of the market. September 2026: Northern Trust partnered with Perpetual (Asia) for full-cycle retail unit-trust servicing — custody, fund administration, middle office and TA, with independent trustee oversight — a month after expanding First Sentier's Singapore unit-trust administration. Integrated fund-servicing demand is broadening from alternatives into the retail fund base.

Market snapshot — the China gateway

  • AUM HK$35.1T (US$4.53T), +13% YoY; net inflows +81% to HK$705B.
  • Deep cross-border plumbing: Stock/Bond/ETF Connect, Mutual Recognition of Funds (enhanced Jan 2025), Wealth Connect.
  • OFC (open-ended fund company) regime growing as the local fund vehicle; ETF market expanding via Connect inclusion.

Regulatory & structural drivers

  • Type 13 depositary regime (Oct 2024): SFC licensing for depositaries of public funds — 19 licensed; creates a new regulated service line.
  • VASP / ASPIRe roadmap (Feb 2025) and VA custody requirements (Aug 2025): consultation toward a standalone virtual-asset custodian regime.
  • HKEX IFRS S2 climate disclosures (FY2025); MPF expanding to active and gold ETFs.
  • JPMorgan's local custody exit made HK a displacement battleground — HSBC/StanChart absorbing flows.
Opportunity: Competitive but growing. The plays: Type 13 depositary servicing, ETF servicing on Connect flows, digital-asset custody under the emerging VA regime, and targeting clients displaced by JPM's local custody exit.

Market snapshot — the superannuation engine

  • A$4.2T superannuation system (~US$2.6T), 4th-largest pension pool globally, growing +11.5% YoY on mandated contributions (SG rate 12% from Jul 2025).
  • Continued mega-fund consolidation — post-merger entities routinely re-tender custody and administration.
  • ACSA league table (Dec 2025): JPMorgan A$1,495B total AUC, Citi A$1,055B, Northern Trust A$995B; BNY ~A$27B (#11).

Regulatory & structural drivers

  • CPS 230 Operational Risk (Jul 2025): critical-operations identification, disruption tolerances, 72-hour incident notification, 20-day offshore arrangement notice — the decade's biggest vendor reassessment.
  • Payday Super (Jul 2026): per-payday contribution processing — a volume surge for registry/TA providers.
  • Funds insourcing investment management but buying technology, data and outsourced trading.
  • Mandatory climate disclosures from 2025; ASIC RG 97 fee-disclosure review.
Opportunity: CPS 230 has opened a time-limited RFP window as every APRA-regulated fund reviews third-party arrangements. Combine with JPM's local custody exit and post-merger re-tenders: Australia is the most catalyst-rich developed market in the region right now.

Market snapshot — the savings-to-investment shift

  • Investment management AUM ¥635.7T (~US$4.2T), +17.1% YoY — record high.
  • New NISA (Jan 2024): permanent, expanded tax-free retail investing — retail fund inflows ¥11.6T in FY2024, +160% YoY.
  • GPIF (~US$1.7T) is the world's largest pension fund; trust banks (MUFG, SMTB, Mizuho legacy) dominate domestic servicing.
  • State Street's Mizuho acquisition (~$850B AUC, completed Oct 2025) consolidated its Japan position — and may trigger client reviews.

Regulatory & structural drivers

  • "Asset Management Nation" policy (2024): government push to open the asset management industry, welcome new entrants, and reform stewardship (3rd Code revision Jun 2025).
  • Stablecoin rules (Jun 2026) and progressive digital-asset framework — Japan is a Tier-1 regulated digital-asset market.
  • Large shareholding reporting reform (May 2026); ISSB disclosures phased from FY2027.
Opportunity: NISA volumes are industrialising retail fund servicing, and new AM entrants under the Asset Management Nation push need full-stack support without trust-bank legacy ties. Post Mizuho–State Street consolidation, some Japanese institutions will want a credible alternative at the table.

Market snapshot — the region's fastest compounder

  • MF AUM ₹85.76 lakh cr (~US$1T, Jul 2026); 5.6x in a decade; 21.3% of GDP (record, still low vs developed markets); SIPs ₹30,000+ cr/month from 9.7 cr accounts; 61 straight months of positive equity inflows.
  • AIF commitments ₹16.94 lakh cr (~$190B, +25.6% YoY, ~30% 5-yr CAGR); 1,829 AIFs vs 732 in 2021; projected ~$247B by 2029.
  • Custodial AUC ₹278.5 trillion (Sep 2024) across 17 SEBI-registered custodians; FPI AUC ₹30+ lakh cr; T+1 live, optional T+0 phasing in.
  • TA duopoly: CAMS (~68%, FY26 rev ₹1,516 cr) and KFintech (~31%, FY26 rev ₹1,301 cr, internationalising across APAC).
  • GIFT City (IFSC): US$26.3B fund commitments; unified regulator (IFSCA); tax-neutral relocation; 100% NRI corpus in IFSC FPIs.

Regulatory & structural drivers

  • SEBI Custodian Regulations overhaul (notified Sep 18, 2025): minimum net worth raised ₹50 cr → ₹75 cr (3-yr transition; ₹100 cr was the original consultation proposal); custodians permitted to offer other financial services (fund accounting et al.) — non-bank custodians via segregated SBUs; governance, BCP/DR and board-committee mandates.
  • AIF reforms: dematerialisation of units, custodian appointment restructured, co-investment schemes (Sep 2025), proposed GARUDA green-channel for faster scheme launches.
  • New vehicles: Specialised Investment Funds (SIF), MF Lite for passives.
  • DDP regime makes the custodian the FPI gateway — win India-entry, win the mandate.
Opportunity: The regulatory door for integrated custody + fund accounting just opened, the CAMS/KFin duopoly is focused on domestic MF rather than AIF/cross-border, and GIFT City is greenfield. Highest competitive-gap score of any P1 market. (A full India-only deep dive exists as a companion briefing.)

Market snapshot — huge, but access-controlled

  • Public fund AUM CNY 32.83T (~US$4.6T) — the largest single AUM pool in the region.
  • Access channels: QFII/RQFII (simplified), Stock/Bond Connect (expanding), 100% foreign-owned AMC licences now granted.
  • Onshore custody dominated by large domestic banks (ICBC, CCB, BOC); foreign servicers operate mainly via HK.

Constraints & posture

  • Data localisation and capital controls limit cross-border operating models.
  • Sub-custody partnerships are essential; direct onshore build is a long-cycle bet.
  • Treat as a flow market serviced from Hong Kong: Connect servicing, QFII custody support for global clients, ETF cross-listings.
Opportunity: Partnership market only in this planning horizon. Monetise China exposure through HK infrastructure and global-client Connect flows rather than onshore expansion.

Market snapshot — the trophy-mandate market

  • Fund industry KRW 1,937T (~US$1.3T, end-2025).
  • NPS — world's 3rd-largest pension — anchors the market: State Street services ~$195B global equities + ~$43B alternatives (custody, accounting, performance, compliance, sec lending, middle office; renewed 2024).
  • KIC (sovereign fund) and major insurers run periodic global custody RFP cycles.

Drivers & posture

  • NPS/KIC mandate cycles and insurance solvency rules drive outsourcing demand.
  • JPMorgan exited local custody in Korea — sub-custody flows moving to regional banks.
  • State Street is deeply entrenched; displacement economics are poor outside RFP windows.
Opportunity: Selective. Track the NPS/KIC RFP calendar and position early for the next cycle; pursue insurers and mid-tier managers meanwhile. A trophy win here re-rates the whole regional franchise.

Market snapshot — structural transformation via TDCC

  • Total fund industry NT$17.5T (~US$536B); retail sub-brokerage volumes +60% YoY.
  • TDCC centralised custody platform: Citi selected as global custody partner (~$153B) — a structural rewiring of how Taiwan accesses global markets.
  • JPMorgan exited local custody in Taiwan — another displacement pocket.

Drivers & posture

  • FSC-driven platform centralisation creates concentrated volume through fewer counterparties.
  • Insurers (large offshore bond books) and ETF issuers are the institutional demand base.
Opportunity: Selective. The TDCC award shows mandates here come via infrastructure partnerships — cultivate TDCC, the FSC and large insurers rather than broad coverage.

Deep dives

State Street — the relationship giant, deepening Japan via Mizuho

Scale: $53.8T AUC/A (YE-2025). APAC HQ Hong Kong; Tokyo, Bangalore, Hangzhou (~1,500 tech staff), Singapore, Sydney, Melbourne, Seoul; 500+ in Japan. Services: custody, fund accounting/admin, TA, ETF baskets, middle/back-office outsourcing, largest securities-lending franchise in APAC, FX, data/analytics; Alpha front-to-back platform (35 clients, 25 live). Anchor: Korea NPS — ~$195B equities + ~$43B alternatives since 2014/2018, renewed 2024.

Strategic moves

  • Mizuho global custody acquisition (Oct 2025): ~$850B AUC + $24B AUA.
  • UOB Asset Management 10-year mandate (2024, "Deal of the Year Asia").
  • Columbia Threadneedle ~$431B AUC/A; JSCC partnership (2025 award); Shariah custody award; acquired CF Global Trading.
  • "Best Global Custodian in APAC" 28 consecutive years (Asia Asset Management).

Weaknesses & gaps

  • Late to digital-asset custody vs StanChart/HSBC.
  • Alpha conversion slow — 25 of 35 clients live.
  • Concentration risk in a few mega-mandates.
  • Less aggressive in India TA / AIF administration.

BNY — biggest globally, lightest in APAC

Scale: $59.3T AUC/A (YE-2025) — the world's largest custodian, but ranked ~#11 in Australia AUC (A$27B) and thin in Asian local custody. Won GPIF data & analytics ("Deal of the Year" 2026) — playing a data/platform game rather than a local-custody game in the region.

Strengths

  • Unmatched global scale, corporate trust and collateral franchises.
  • Data platform ambitions; GPIF relationship is a marquee beachhead in Japan.

Weaknesses & gaps

  • Under-indexed APAC custody share; lost EPF Malaysia (~$40B) to JPMorgan in 2025.
  • Corporate-trust strength less differentiated in APAC than the US.
  • Limited local sub-custody footprint — dependent on partners.

Citi — the proprietary-network competitor

Scale: ~$29.2T AUC; #1 in direct custody & clearing globally (26.8% share) — the widest proprietary local-custody network, which it is monetising as others retreat. #3 in Australia (A$1,055B), adding 24 new Australian clients in 18 months.

Strategic moves

  • TDCC Taiwan centralised platform global custody mandate (~$153B, 2024).
  • Global X ETFs HK fund services ("Deal of the Year" 2025); BetaShares custody + fund admin; Fiducian custody/admin/registry.
  • Positioning ETF servicing and mid-market Australia as growth engines.

Weaknesses & gaps

  • Fund administration and TA depth thinner than its custody network.
  • Less established in private-markets/GP-LP servicing.
  • Group-level restructuring can distract from securities services investment.

JPMorgan — retreating locally, winning globally

Scale: ~$35T AUC (end-2024). #1 in Australia total AUC (A$1,495B). Exited local custody in HK, Taiwan, Australia and Korea — outsourcing ~$520B of sub-custody to HSBC and StanChart — while still winning global mandates: EPF Malaysia (~$40B, displacing BNY, "Deal of the Year" 2025) and Income Insurance Singapore ("Custody Deal of the Year" 2026). Fusion is its data platform play.

Strengths

  • Balance-sheet strength and global custody scale; markets/financing adjacencies.
  • Still the name to beat in global custody RFPs for large asset owners.

Weaknesses & gaps

  • Local custody exit creates dependency on HSBC/StanChart — and displaced clients in four markets.
  • Not seen as a digital-asset custody leader.
  • Fusion not yet deeply embedded in APAC.

Northern Trust — the asset-owner and private-markets specialist

Scale: $18.7T AUC/A (YE-2025); #4 in Australia (A$995B, A$236B AUA). Franchise built on asset owners (pensions, sovereigns) and alternatives servicing (>$1T in private markets globally). Recent APAC signal: Fiji National Provident Fund custody win (2025), framed as a "major milestone" for its APAC commitment.

Strengths

  • Genuine private-markets servicing leadership; high-touch asset-owner model.
  • Strong Australia asset-owner base.

Weaknesses & gaps

  • Smaller scale and thinner Asia (ex-Australia) footprint than the big three.
  • Limited retail/TA capability in Asia; not a local sub-custody player.

Where the market is underserved

Six structural gaps — from Deloitte's Global Asset Servicing Survey 2025, EY analysis and the competitive read — each a potential growth vector.

Gap 1 · Front-to-back platform modernisation

EY: only 33% of asset servicers fully fund digital initiatives; just 21% have digitalised most workflows. State Street's Alpha has 35 clients (25 live) — slow conversion. Room exists for a genuinely technology-forward alternative.

Gap 2 · Private-markets servicing

APAC alternatives AUM exceeds $1T in Singapore alone (+14% YoY); India AIF at ~$190B. Most global custodians are optimised for traditional fund flows, not GP-LP structures, waterfalls and private-asset valuation. Northern Trust leads but the market is far from saturated.

Gap 3 · Digital-asset custody

SG DTSP (Jun 2025), HK VASP/ASPIRe (Feb 2025), Japan stablecoin rules (Jun 2026): regulated digital-asset custody is a greenfield. StanChart and HSBC are early movers; the big three are relatively late. First-mover advantage is still available.

Gap 4 · Real-time data & AI/analytics

Real-time data and AI are top client pressure points (Deloitte). JPM Fusion and BNY's data platform are early but not deeply embedded in APAC. Clients want integrated data solutions, not custody reports; AI/RPA moving from pilot to live in Singapore fund admin.

Gap 5 · India AIF & GIFT City fund services

AIF commitments ~$190B, projected ~$247B by 2029. SEBI's Sep 2025 overhaul permits custodians to offer fund accounting — opening integrated custody + admin. The CAMS/KFin duopoly is focused on domestic MF, not AIF/cross-border. GIFT City is underpenetrated by global servicers.

Gap 6 · Local sub-custody vacuum

JPMorgan's exit from local custody in HK, Taiwan, Australia and Korea created a vacuum. HSBC/StanChart are absorbing flows, but clients wanting a custodian with local presence and global capability are underserved — a direct opening via local investment or strategic partnership.

Industry pressure points to weaponise in pitches: fee compression forcing value-add differentiation · talent/digital-funding shortfalls at competitors (33%/21% EY stats) · client shift from transactional custody to integrated data platforms · multi-jurisdiction regulatory burden · alternatives-servicing demand outstripping capable supply.

Buying-cycle characteristics

  • Cycle length: 9–24 months for mega-mandates; 3–9 months mid-market.
  • Process: RFP → shortlist (3–5) → due diligence → BAFO → contract → 6–18 month transition.
  • Retention: custody is sticky (90%+), but large mandates re-tender every 5–7 years.
  • Triggers: regulation, mergers, leadership change, service failures, JPM-exit displacement.
  • Switching costs: high — data migration, re-platforming, legal restructuring. Use this defensively once landed.
