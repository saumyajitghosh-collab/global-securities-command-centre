Why this matters for an APAC sales strategy

Structural demand

Financialisation of household savings is the deepest secular trend in Indian finance: SIPs turned retail flows into an annuity. Fund launches (61 straight months of positive equity inflows, heavy NFO activity) mean continuous demand for fund accounting, TA onboarding, and custody at scale.

Underserved sophistication

The fastest-growing pools — AIFs, PMS, GIFT City funds, private credit — need institutional-grade administration, investor servicing, and cross-border custody. This is precisely where global providers' playbooks (Luxembourg, Singapore, Dublin) transplant well.

Two-way corridor

India is both a destination (FPI flows into a US$4–5tn market cap) and an origin (outbound feeders, NRI wealth via GIFT City, Indian managers going offshore). A regional head can sell India access to global clients and global reach to Indian managers.

Fee pressure is real

Domestic custody and TA are brutally price-competitive. The margin is in bundling: custody + fund accounting + middle office + data. Selling single-product custody in India is a race to the bottom; selling the stack is not.

Folio 03 · The Market

Custody & FPI servicing

The most contested layer — 17 licensed custodians, a mature DDP regime for foreign investors, and one of the world's fastest settlement cycles.

How the market is structured STRUCTURE

Custody in India splits into three books: FPI custody (foreign portfolio investors accessing Indian securities — the historic stronghold of foreign bank custodians like Citi, Deutsche Bank, HSBC, Standard Chartered, BNP Paribas, DBS); domestic institutional custody (mutual funds, AIFs, PMS, insurance — where domestic players like SBI-SG, ICICI Bank, Kotak Mahindra, Axis Bank, HDFC Bank, Stock Holding Corporation and Orbis compete hard on price); and clearing/settlement services bundled as custodians act as clearing members for their clients.

The Designated Depository Participant (DDP) regime makes the custodian the front door for foreign investors: SEBI delegates FPI registration processing to DDPs, and regulation requires the DDP and custodian to be the same entity. Winning the DDP relationship at fund inception locks in custody — which makes FPI custody a land-grab at the point of India market entry.

The FPI book and market infrastructure SCALE

FPI assets under custody have run above ₹30 lakh crore, riding a market whose capitalisation crossed US$4 trillion. Retail depth is extraordinary — CDSL alone reported 10 million+ new depository accounts in a single year — and India completed its T+1 settlement migration ahead of the US and Europe, with optional T+0 being phased in for top securities. Custodians describe the resulting agenda plainly: API connectivity, microservices architecture, and batch-less processing so offshore clients can operate on Indian timelines.

Watch item: FPI flows are volatile (record FPI selling in early 2026 was absorbed by record domestic MF buying). The structural story is the domestic bid; the FPI book is cyclical.

What buyers actually score custodians on BUYING CRITERIA

FPIs / global managers: DDP onboarding speed, tax and regulatory handholding (India's FPI compliance is document-heavy), cut-off times and FX efficiency under T+1, single-window APAC coverage, and quality of corporate-actions data. Domestic AMCs/insurers: price, SEBI compliance track record, integration with RTAs and fund accounting, and increasingly digital dashboards. AIF managers: mandatory custodian appointment (now effectively across categories) plus willingness to service complex/unlisted assets.

T+1 → T+0
Among the fastest settlement regimes globally; operational alpha for custodians with real-time architecture.

Folio 04 · The Market

Fund administration

The growth engine. Mutual fund accounting is mature and cheap; the alternatives wave — AIFs, PMS, private credit, GIFT funds — is where administration revenue is being created.

Mutual funds — scale, thin margin

  • ~59 AMCs; AUM ₹87.08 lakh cr (Aug 2026, +1.5% MoM on record SIP flows of ₹32,297 cr), on course for ₹100 lakh cr within a couple of years.
  • Fund accounting historically done in-house or by custodian-affiliated shops; heavily commoditised.
  • Angle: co-sourcing, oversight platforms, data services and NAV contingency rather than displacing core accounting on price.

Alternatives — the real prize

  • AIF commitments ₹16.94 lakh cr, +25.6% YoY; Category II (PE, private credit, real estate) dominates with 1,019 of 1,829 funds.
  • SIFs (Specialized Investment Funds) hit ₹31,175 cr net assets in Aug 2026 — more than doubled from ₹13,814 cr in May. ₹7,699 cr net inflows in a single month; folios up from ~94,000 to 125,000+. Derivatives-enabled long-short and hybrid strategies create servicing requirements (collateral, margin, derivatives lifecycle, exposure monitoring) far beyond vanilla MF ops.
  • Managers are small teams that outsource by necessity: accounting, capital calls, distributions, waterfall calc, investor reporting.
  • Global admins (IQ-EQ, Apex et al.) have entered onshore and in GIFT City — validation that international-standard admin sells here.
SIF scale acceleration → Derivatives / Collateral / Fund-Accounting Servicing Review. SIFs are operationally closer to an alternatives-lite, derivatives-enabled managed-fund model than a vanilla mutual fund. Long-short equity and hybrid strategies create heavier requirements around collateral, derivatives lifecycle, valuation, margin, cash management, fund accounting, reconciliation, exposure monitoring and investor reporting. The pitch is not "we can administer a SIF" — it is custody + derivatives servicing + collateral/margin + NAV/fund accounting + exposure controls + investor servicing + regulatory reporting as one proposition. Target universe: AMCs launching SIF franchises, incumbent MF custodians, fund accountants, collateral/margin providers, RTAs and middle-office providers. ASSESSMENT

Transfer agency — a duopoly

Registrar & Transfer Agency for mutual funds is one of the most concentrated markets in global asset servicing. Understand it to know what not to attack head-on.

CAMS — market share
~68%

CAMS

  • Services MF AUM above ₹55 lakh cr; client list skews to the largest AMCs (incl. 10 of the top 15).
  • FY26 revenue ₹1,516 cr, EBITDA margin ~46.5% — evidence of pricing power from switching costs.
  • Diversified into AIF/PMS servicing (50%+ share there), NPS CRA, insurance repository, payments, account aggregation.

KFin Technologies

  • Services 31 of 59 AMCs; growing faster off a smaller base — FY26 revenue ₹1,301 cr, +19.3% YoY.
  • Internationalising aggressively: TA and fund admin in Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Hong Kong, Thailand, Canada, and GIFT City.
  • Positions as platform/SaaS: digital onboarding, fund accounting and admin for global managers — i.e., a potential competitor and partner across APAC.
Strategic read: Do not pitch head-on MF transfer agency in India — switching costs and per-folio economics favour the incumbents, and both are embedded in AMC operations. The openings are: (1) TA for AIFs, PMS and GIFT City funds where institutional investors demand global-standard investor servicing; (2) cross-border TA for Indian managers distributing offshore; (3) partnership or white-label plays — note KFin is already selling into your APAC markets, so expect to meet them in Singapore and HK deals too.

GIFT City — India's offshore centre onshore

The IFSC at GIFT City is India's deliberate answer to Singapore, Dubai and Luxembourg: a tax-neutral, dollar-denominated fund jurisdiction with its own unified regulator, IFSCA.

US$45.1 bn
Commitments raised by GIFT funds — up from $39.1bn in Mar 2026; retail investor base +146% in the June quarter.
IFSCA · Q1 FY27 (Jun 2026)
1
Unified regulator (IFSCA) replacing RBI/SEBI/IRDAI/PFRDA inside the zone — faster, principles-based.
100%
NRI/OCI contribution now permitted in IFSC-regulated FPI corpus (up from 50%) — unlocking the diaspora wealth corridor.

What's being built there

  • Fund Management Regulations (updated 2025): venture schemes, restricted (non-retail) schemes, retail schemes, family investment funds.
  • Inbound feeders (global money → Indian MFs/ETFs, e.g., Tata, Mirae, Sundaram retail GIFT funds; 20+ retail funds, US$2bn+ AUM by Dec 2025) and outbound feeders (Indian money → global assets via LRS/OPI).
  • Offshore fund relocation to IFSC on a tax-neutral basis — a direct pitch to Mauritius/Singapore legacy structures.
  • Global admins and service firms (IQ-EQ, Apex, KFin and peers) already licensed on the ground.

Why it matters to a global securities-services firm

  • Greenfield mandates: every GIFT fund needs custody, admin, TA — no 25-year incumbent relationships to displace.
  • Familiar law: IFSCA borrows from developed-market rulebooks; your Luxembourg/Singapore operating model largely transplants.
  • Digital-asset optionality: IFSC is India's sandbox for tokenised assets and digital-asset servicing while the mainland stays conservative.
  • Political will: sustained government sponsorship and ease-of-doing-business pushes from both SEBI and IFSCA.

Folio 08 · The Battlefield

Regulatory map

Four regulators shape the addressable market; the direction of travel is consistently pro-deepening.

SEBI — securities markets, funds, custodians, RTAs PRIMARY

Regulates custodians (Custodian Regulations), FPIs (FPI Regulations, DDP regime), mutual funds, AIFs (AIF Regulations — Cat I/II/III, mandatory custodians, dematerialisation of units), PMS, and RTAs. Current agenda: ease-of-doing-business reforms, GARUDA green-channel for AIF launches, accredited-investor expansion (base up ~300% YoY to 2,773), T+0 optional settlement, and continued FPI disclosure tightening. SEBI leadership publicly frames AIFs as a "pillar of capital markets" — expect facilitation, not friction.

IFSCA — everything inside GIFT City GATEWAY

Unified regulator under its own Act (2019). Fund Management Regulations govern FMEs and schemes; separate frameworks cover banking units, capital-market intermediaries and (progressively) digital assets. Tax design — exemptions for specified funds, tax-neutral fund relocation, concessional regimes — is a core part of the sell. If you serve funds in Singapore or Dubai, IFSCA's rulebook will feel familiar by design.

RBI — banking, FX, and the rupee MACRO

Governs bank custodians' banking activities, FX conversion for FPI flows, FEMA rules on cross-border investment, and the cash leg of settlement. Rupee volatility and FX cut-offs under T+1 are recurring FPI pain points — a service differentiator for custodians with strong FX desks and extended processing windows.

PFRDA & IRDAI — pensions and insurance pools POOLS

PFRDA oversees the National Pension System (multi-trillion-rupee and compounding; CRA record-keeping is a licensed function — CAMS entered as a CRA). IRDAI governs insurers, whose investment books require custody and valuation services under tight investment regulations. Both are long-duration institutional pools that anchor multi-year servicing mandates.

Compliance note for sales: India rewards regulatory presence. Mandates increasingly require onshore licensed entities (custodian registration, RTA registration, IFSCA licences). The sales strategy and the licence strategy must move together — flag entity/licence gaps to Product and Legal in the first 30 days.

Folio 10 · The Play

Where to hunt

Ranked by fit for a global securities-services franchise: revenue density × incumbency gap × regulatory openness.

1 · Alternatives full-stack

₹16.94 lakh cr of AIF commitments growing ~25–30% a year, plus SIFs more than doubling to ₹31,175 cr in three months (May–Aug 2026). Run by lean GP teams and AMC SIF franchises with global LPs. with global LPs. Bundle admin + TA + custody + depositary-style oversight. Highest win-rate white space; deal sizes grow with each vintage.

2 · GIFT City greenfield

Every new FME needs the full stack with no incumbent to displace. First-mover service relationships here compound the way Luxembourg relationships did in the 1990s.

3 · Middle-office & data outsourcing

Almost entirely in-house today across AMCs and insurers. IBOR, performance, risk, regulatory reporting as a service — premium fees, no domestic incumbent, and a natural wedge into full outsourcing.

4 · Cross-border corridors

Inbound: FPI/DDP capture at India-entry (sold via global network-management relationships). Outbound: Indian AMCs' offshore funds and GIFT feeders needing global custody/TA. NRI wealth via IFSC (100% NRI corpus rule) is a fresh corridor.

5 · Insurance & pension mandates

Long-duration pools (NPS compounding at high teens) with tender-driven procurement. Lower margin, but anchors scale and signals commitment to the market.

6 · Digital-asset servicing (optionality)

APAC digital-asset custody is formalising fast (Japan, HK regimes live). India's onshore stance is conservative, but IFSC is the sanctioned sandbox — plant the flag early, monetise later.

Talking points that land

Numbers and framings for client meetings, panels and internal advocacy — each with its as-of date.

The scale story OPENERS

"India's mutual fund industry manages roughly a trillion US dollars — ₹85.8 lakh crore as of July 2026 — and it got there 5.6x from a decade ago. SIP investors alone commit over ₹30,000 crore every month, and equity funds have seen positive net inflows for over five consecutive years. This is not a flows story; it's an infrastructure story."

The alternatives story AIF / GP MEETINGS

"AIF commitments crossed ₹16.9 lakh crore in March 2026 — up 25% in a year, ~30% CAGR over five. There are 1,829 registered AIFs versus 732 five years ago, and SEBI is proposing green-channel launches under GARUDA. Fund formation is accelerating faster than operational capacity — that's exactly the gap we close."

The GIFT story CROSS-BORDER

"GIFT City funds have raised over US$26 billion in commitments. With a unified regulator, tax-neutral relocation for offshore funds, and 100% NRI participation now allowed in IFSC FPIs, India has built an onshore-offshore centre. We can lift our Singapore and Luxembourg operating model straight into it."

The infrastructure story OPS / COO MEETINGS

"India settled the move to T+1 before the US did and is piloting optional T+0. Market cap has crossed US$4 trillion with tens of millions of new depository accounts. Operating here now demands API-first, batch-less servicing — which is precisely the modernisation conversation we want to be having with every client's operations team."

Objection handling DEFENCE

"Domestic providers are cheaper." — True at the line-item level. We compete on total operating cost across markets, data quality, and what your global investors and regulators require — one platform from Mumbai to Sydney.

"The RTA duopoly has it locked." — For domestic mutual funds, yes. We're not bidding for that. Alternatives, cross-border and GIFT funds are being won on institutional standards, and those mandates are being decided right now.

"FPI flows just turned negative." — And domestic mutual funds bought a record ₹1 lakh crore in the same month. The domestic bid now cushions the foreign cycle — which makes the servicing revenue more durable, not less.
